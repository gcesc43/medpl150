The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It


The interactive pages of “The Rising Risk & How We Stop It” are effective in setting the tone and sending the message that the opinion piece is to be taken seriously. When watched in full screen and in a dark room, as I experienced it, the colors and language work as a call to action, urging the readers to care about the looming nuclear war. At first, I thought the interactive designs would end once the journalistic article started, and I was already impressed with the first intro page. Therefore, I was pleasantly surprised when I saw the article had other interactive sections.

The graphics that moved as I scrolled and read helped me focus on what I was reading in a way that I usually do not. In a world of fast-paced clips and mechanical voiceover narrations, it can be hard to focus on what one is reading when there are no visual stimulus. This article takes the visual aspects one step further and manages to create an immersive yet not-overwhelming world that kept me replaying the same parts and rereading the same sentences over and over again. By the time I read the rest of the article, I was able to take in the information from an emotional place that is far more memorable than reading just from an analytical perspective.

As someone who was not familiar with the politics or threat of nuclear war, this article presented the data and information in a way that I was able to take in, follow, and understand. Although unsettling, the graphics forced me to realize that some issues that I thought lived in the past, such as the global threat of nuclear war, are a lot more pressing than I once thought. The number of warheads in the United States as recently as 2023 further emphasizes the urgency of the matter. Another aspect that helped me understand the conflict better and grasp the tone of the article was the GIFs that play throughout the article. I appreciate these because they put a face to names and quotes. I am unsure whether this is the result of my not reading enough news online, but I had never seen quotes and sources presented in this format, so I found it to be a clever, simple, and efficient tool that more platforms should adopt.

Overall, I was glad to read the article. I had never read a newspaper article presented like his, and I found this way of presenting information to be more memorable than most things I usually read online. The colors, style of graphics, and timing work in harmony to tell a story while presenting relevant information at the same time. As someone who has always been interested in social sciences, humanities, arts, filmmaking, and communications, I liked that this article introduced me to a new way of conveying information that combines storytelling and cinematography with non-fiction storytelling, journalism, and politics.
