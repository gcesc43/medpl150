Reading Reflection - Week 2

On Photography by Susan Sontag


According to Sontag, “photographs are as much an interpretation of the world as paintings and drawings are.” When the photographer takes a picture, they are capturing a moment in time as it happens. However, they use their taste and conscience to frame a photo, light it, and choose what to capture and what to leave out. Therefore, while images are powerful tools that can give people access to a different reality and evoke feelings of pleasure, nostalgia, empathy, and so on, they can only be truly understood when considering their historical and societal context.

Although Sontag’s “On Photography” was published in 1977, her conclusion about how we’ve come to use photographs has only become more relevant with the digital age and social media. She wrote, “Ultimately, having an experience becomes identical with taking a photograph of it, and participating in a public event comes more and more to be equivalent to looking at it in photographed form… Today everything exists to end in a photograph.” This resonated with me because, as someone born in 2001, I grew up having my picture taken and always loved capturing memories.

As I started gaining some independence, and my parents could not be there to photograph every event in my life, every field trip, road trip, sleepover, and birthday party became the perfect opportunity for my friends and me to beg our parents to borrow their point-and-shoot cameras or buy us our own disposable cameras. We later grew up to experience the boom of smartphones and social media, and what started as a fun way to save memories spiraled into what can only be called hoarding records of every insignificant "event" in our lives, which was only an event because we turned it into one by photographing it. When we started posting on Instagram, everything from breakfast to the walk home became an event worth photographing and sharing. That led me to eventually burn out and take a break from taking pictures, even though it was something I really love doing. Since then, I made my social media private and started taking pictures on my phone as a hobby, rather than to post them on social media.

Since I started making films, about 4 years ago, and bought a DSLR camera, I became more intentional with my photography. I like to travel, and when I do, I focus on taking pictures of landscapes, architecture, art, and other details that I think make the place come alive and give it its character. Similarly, I like to take portraits of people and focus on their facial expressions and characteristic features. I still like having my picture taken as a way to preserve memories, but I try to be more selective about what is worth saving.

To visually represent the notions of our current world through photography, illustration, or the combination of image and text, I would probably do an experimental collage that juxtaposes nature and technology themes. Lately, I’ve been seeing a lot of ads in the Subway that advertise AI programs, Face Tune, and other mobile applications that seem very damaging for the environment, people's self-image, and the job market. I would use irony and mimic the format and language of these ads in my collage to bring attention to how straightforwardly dangerous these programs and advertisements are.
